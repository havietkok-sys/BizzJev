# Semantic Annotation Guide (Independent Replication)

You are annotating consumer complaint narratives about debt collection. For each narrative, decide
for each of the six concepts below whether that concept is **actually represented in the narrative
text**. Judge only what the text expresses — not what is legally proven, not what seems most
important, and not which category "best describes" the complaint as a whole.

- **YES** — the concept is expressed in the narrative (directly or indirectly but clearly).
- **NO** — the concept is not expressed.
- **UNCLEAR** — you cannot tell from the text.

Multiple concepts may be YES for the same narrative. Do not force a single category.

## The six concepts (neutral descriptions)

1. **Identity theft / unauthorized use of identity**
   The debt exists because someone used this person's identity or personal information without
   permission — e.g., an account was opened or purchases were made without their knowledge, their
   information was stolen or misused, or they filed an identity-theft/police report describing
   fraudulent accounts. (A simple "I don't owe this" without any claim of unauthorized use of
   their identity is a different idea.)

2. **Missing or inadequate debt information**
   The person asked for (or was entitled to) information or documentation about the debt and did
   not receive it, or what they received was too limited to verify the debt — e.g., ignored
   requests, no response, documents missing, only statements or printouts instead of the
   underlying agreement or itemization.

3. **Wrong amount**
   The amount being collected is claimed to be incorrect — e.g., higher than actually owed,
   improper fees/interest added, inconsistent figures given by different sources, charges for
   something never received or never ordered, payments not credited, or a balance that should be
   zero because it was already paid or settled.

4. **Threatened credit damage**
   The collector said or implied that the person's credit would be harmed (e.g., reported to
   credit bureaus, score ruined) because of the debt or as pressure to pay. (An account merely
   already being on a credit report, with no threatened or implied further consequence, is a
   different idea.)

5. **Threatened arrest or jail**
   The collector said or implied the person could be arrested, jailed, or locked up over the debt.
   (Lawsuits, wage garnishment, credit reporting, property seizure, or rude/hostile behavior
   without any threat to personal liberty are different ideas.)

6. **Sued without proper notification**
   A lawsuit was actually filed and the person was not properly notified — e.g., never served,
   served at the wrong address or on the wrong person, learned of the case only later (default
   judgment, garnishment, seized refund), or otherwise got no notice of the case. (A threat of a
   future lawsuit, a case the person knew about and participated in, or the person's own lawsuit
   against the collector are different ideas.)

## Practical notes

- Read the whole narrative; relevant statements may appear anywhere.
- The parenthetical "different idea" hints are guides, not exhaustive rules.
- If two concepts are both genuinely present, mark both YES.
- If the text is too thin or garbled to tell, use UNCLEAR rather than guessing.
